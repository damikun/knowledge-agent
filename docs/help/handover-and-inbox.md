# Handover and the Inbox

When a visitor needs a person, the request reaches your team in the [Inbox](/inbox), and also by email or in Slack when you set them up on [Notifications](/notifications).

## How a visitor reaches a person

- The widget decides whether a person can be offered at all: Talk to a person in the widget's settings ([Widgets](/widgets)).
- The agent decides when it offers one: never, only when the visitor asks, or also when it cannot answer. Set it on the agent's Handover tab ([Agents](/agents)).

While the request waits, the visitor can keep chatting with the assistant. When nobody joins in time (15 minutes by default), the request expires and the Inbox marks it as a missed request; you can still reply later.

## Work in the Inbox

1. Open the [Inbox](/inbox). Waiting shows who waits for a person; Mine and Unassigned show who owns what.
2. Open a conversation and press Take over to join, or just reply: replying joins too.
3. Use the suggested reply to draft an answer from your sources, then edit and send it.
4. Press Back to the assistant to let the agent answer again, or Close conversation when it is done.

Assign a conversation to a colleague from its details. The number next to Inbox in the menu counts the visitors waiting.

## Email and Slack

- Email is two-way: the transcript goes to your support address, and replies to it come back into the chat. Set SMTP and IMAP on [Notifications](/notifications).
- Slack posts each request to a channel with Take, Back to the assistant and Close buttons; replies in its thread reach the visitor. Set the bot on [Notifications](/notifications).

Each widget chooses where its requests go: the Inbox only, email or Slack. When the chosen way is not set up, the request falls back to email, then the Inbox.

## Feedback from visitors

Visitors rate answers with thumbs and rate the whole chat when it ends. See them per widget and agent on [Feedback](/feedback).
